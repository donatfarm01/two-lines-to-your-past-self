Two Lines to Your Past Self — build notes

The bounty asks for exactly two lines, one to 280 characters in total, written to a file. The first line must warn the writer's past self about one specific moment, and the second must say in one sentence why that moment mattered and what changed because of it. A judge model compares entries two at a time and weighs the concreteness of the moment first, then how clearly the second line traces cause and effect.

The entry I chose is a small scene with a sharp turn. The first line names a time, a place and a decision you can picture: the last bus home on the night the river rises, one more drink, the girl in the yellow coat. The second line does not restate the warning; it lands the consequence twice over, the bus never crossing the bridge and the girl becoming the reason the writer never leaves town. The voice is plain and written to one specific person, with no wasted words and no cleverness for its own sake.

The program is a short Python script with no network calls and no secrets. It holds the two lines as constants, checks that the joined text is non-empty, at most 280 characters, and exactly two lines, then writes it to the output path. Verification ran the program twice and confirmed the file holds exactly two lines inside the gate, with matching hashes on both runs.
